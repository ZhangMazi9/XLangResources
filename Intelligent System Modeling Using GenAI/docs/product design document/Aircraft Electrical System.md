# Aircraft Electrical System
This paper takes the aircraft electrical system as the research object and proposes a method for the MBSE (Model-Based Systems Engineering) full-process design of the aircraft electrical system using X language. X language is a new-generation integrated modeling and simulation language for complex systems that supports MBSE. It enables the connection between the first three levels and the physical level, thereby realizing the unified modeling of system architecture and physical characteristics.

In the specific design process, this paper first analyzes the requirements and functions of the aircraft electrical system, and obtains a top-level logical model of the system that can be verified through simulation. Next, under the guidance of the top-level model, the modeling of physical levels such as electrical equipment and power supply equipment in the electrical system is carried out. The aircraft electrical system is a typical complex system, and its design process involves the interaction of multiple disciplines such as mechanics and electromagnetism.

During the flight of an actual aircraft, in attitudes such as climbing, level flight, and descending, the power consumption of the system varies due to changes in the working status of various electrical equipment. This research focuses on the interaction between the motion control part and the power-consuming part during flight, and divides the aircraft electrical system into three major parts: flight scenario control, electrical equipment, and power supply equipment. It is further divided into six modules: flight scenario control module, control bus module, radar module, steering gear module, thrust module, and power supply module.

The flight scenario control module is used to design the flight trajectory of the aircraft, enabling it to fly along a predetermined path, and consists of three state machines: level flight, climbing, and descending. The control bus module is used to parse scenario control commands and send them to the corresponding electrical equipment, and is composed of a receiving module and a bus parsing module. The radar module mainly focuses on the current change after it is turned on, which causes the change of power, and is composed of a receiving module and a current transmission module. The steering gear module is used to simulate the automatic driving flight process of the aircraft, and its different states correspond to different flight attitudes, consisting of an inductor coil and a resistor-capacitor module. The thrust module is used to simulate the power output process. The power supply module is used to supply power to electrical equipment, consisting of a signal receiver and a voltage output module. These modules interact and couple with each other, enabling the aircraft to complete the scheduled flight mission.
